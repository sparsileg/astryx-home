Kansha Walkthrough

Kansha keeps your household accounts on your own computer: checking, savings, cards, loans, and investments in one encrypted book, with no subscription and no account to sign up for. Insights is the home page: net worth at three year-ends, this month's income and spending, what's due in the next two weeks, and whether anything needs attention. Every screen in this walkthrough shows Kansha's built-in sample data.

1 / 13

Coming from Quicken? Export one QIF file and Kansha reads your accounts, categories, tags, payees, securities, prices, and every transaction, with both sides of each transfer matched up. Nothing changes until you've seen this summary, and you can test the import first, or take a whole import back out later.

2 / 13

Each account has a register: one transaction per row, the running balance down the right, and the entry line at the bottom. It works entirely from the keyboard. Scheduled and future-dated transactions sit below the line at today in italics, so next week's balance is already in view.

3 / 13

One paycheck, several destinations. A split divides a transaction across categories and transfers: gross pay, federal and state tax, and part sent straight to savings. Remainder shows a check mark when the lines add up.

4 / 13

Paychecks, rent, and bills come again and again. Make each one a schedule, and Kansha reminds you when it's due or enters it by itself. An estimated bill, like electricity, can follow the average of its last few payments.

5 / 13

When something comes due, it's waiting when you open the book: enter it, skip it, or edit it first. Overdue items are flagged, and an estimate is marked so you check the amount against the real bill.

6 / 13

The Calendar puts every scheduled transaction on the day it falls. Tick Projected balance and each day shows what the account will hold at the end of it: the quickest way to see whether a bill lands before the paycheck that covers it.

7 / 13

Reconciling checks an account against its statement. Enter the statement's date and ending balance, tick what it shows, and when the difference reaches zero, finish. From then on you know Kansha and the bank agree up to that date.

8 / 13

Reports cover spending, net worth, investing, and tax. Pick a date range, expand or collapse the categories, and click any amount to see the transactions behind it. Save the reports you run often, and export them to CSV or PDF.

9 / 13

The Investments screen shows every holding in every account at today's prices: shares, market value, gain or loss, and the day's change. Price download is optional, and when it's on, only ticker symbols ever leave your computer.

10 / 13

Kansha keeps every lot with its acquisition date and cost basis. When you sell, choose the lots yourself or let the lot method pick them, and capital gains come out right at tax time.

11 / 13

Spent something away from the computer? kansha add records a purchase from a terminal, and entry files can come from a script or a phone. They wait here until you approve them, and one with a category Kansha doesn't know is flagged before it touches your accounts.

12 / 13

The book is encrypted with your passphrase, and Kansha backs it up by itself: when it closes, a few minutes after a change, and before anything big such as an import. Restoring shows the backup beside the current book, account by account, before anything is replaced.
